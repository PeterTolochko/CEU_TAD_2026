# Employment attention: teaching codebook

**Concept.** Attention to employment and the labour market.

**Unit.** One sentence. Label a sentence `1` when it substantively discusses paid employment, unemployment, hiring, job loss, labour-market conditions, wages, working hours, employers, or employment-related training. Otherwise label it `0`.

## Include

- Creating, losing, finding, or keeping paid jobs.
- Hiring, layoffs, unemployment, vacancies, wages, hours, and paychecks.
- Workers, employers, apprenticeships, or training when the sentence concerns employment or the labour market.
- Negated or uncertain claims about employment. A sentence can discuss employment even when it says a policy will not change it.

## Exclude

- `job` as a task or technical process.
- `work` as effort, volunteering, repair, or a generic activity without an employment context.
- `hire` when it names a specific appointment that is not a labour-market claim.
- `employ` when it means use a method or tool.

## Decision rule

Use the surrounding sentence to disambiguate a word. Do not infer a topic from a keyword alone. Record uncertain cases and resolve them consistently before scoring a larger corpus.

## Scope

This codebook measures attention, not approval. A sentence may support, oppose, predict, or deny an employment outcome and still receive the same topic label.
